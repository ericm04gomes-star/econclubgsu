Panthers and Cubs

A mentorship program for underclassmen

Underclassmen are paired with upperclassmen and graduate students who have already made the decisions they are facing.

How it works

Paired with someone a few years ahead of you

Each cub is matched with a panther, an upperclassman or graduate student in economics. Pairs decide between themselves how often to meet and what to work on. The program runs alongside official university advising rather than replacing it.

Enroll in the program

What mentors help with

The questions that are difficult to raise in a fifteen-minute advising appointment.

Typically covered
  • Course selection and sequencing
  • CV and resume review
  • Which electives are worth taking
  • When to approach faculty about research
  • Preparing for internships and graduate study

For underclassmen

You do not need a plan before enrolling. Most cubs join precisely because they are unsure which direction to take the major, and a mentor is more useful early than late.

For upperclassmen and graduate students

Mentoring is a modest commitment and one of the more direct ways to contribute to the club. It also gives you something concrete to describe on an application.

Attend one meeting before deciding.

Attendance does not require membership, and there are no dues.
